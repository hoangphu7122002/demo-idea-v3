## Question

How does a reader who is not a developer correct a transcript, and how is the correction reviewed and attributed? Decide: the "suggest a correction" path from a Segment on the site (prefilled GitHub web edit, prefilled issue, or both), what a reviewer checks (against the audio at the timestamp), who may merge, how corrections change a Segment's status, how contributors are credited under the open data licence, how disputes are settled, and what the notice-and-takedown and right-of-reply channels look like for v1 (the legal research expects the first notices to concern labels, not copyright, but the channel must exist from launch).

Depends on the file format fixed by the data-model ticket. Keep readers anonymous: no account needed to read, and no opinion data collected.

**Inputs from the data model (#14, resolved).** A correction is an edit to `transcript.md`: the text paragraph of a Segment, or its header line (`### s1093 marine-tondelier 02:08:54.740 --> 02:09:10.020 text:draft speaker:draft overlap`) to change the speaker, set a status to `verified`, split or merge within one Speaker's turn. Allowed and forbidden edits, the id counter and the retired-id map are in `docs/data-model.md` and ADR 0005 (draft PR #35). Verifying a Segment's text also deletes its entry in `words.json`; CI validates every pull request against `schemas/`, so the review question is only whether the change is right against the replay at the timestamp, not whether the file still parses. Git is the corrections log: no verifier or date is stored in the file.
